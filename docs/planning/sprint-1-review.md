\# Shelf — Sprint 1 Review



\## Sprint Goal

A reader can visually manage their entire shelf, adding, viewing, editing, deleting, and moving books between reading stages, with zero data loss during a session.



\## What's actually demoable

Nothing yet. Zero of the 5 Sprint 1 stories have working code behind them. This is an honest gap between the Sprint Goal and where the sprint actually stands.



\## What did happen

\- Real technical planning was completed for 2 of the 5 stories (14 total points committed):

&#x20; - "See a board representing my whole shelf" — Flutter widget structure decided (Column widgets in a scrollable Row, ListView.builder per column, empty-state handling), documented in docs/planning/sprint-1-prep-notes.md.

&#x20; - "Add a book card with a title and author" — data shape and form validation approach decided, documented in the same file.

\- These 2 stories are marked In Progress in Asana, reflecting genuine prep work, not code.

\- The remaining 3 stories (move a card, edit a card, delete a card) are untouched, still To Do.



\## Gap against the Sprint Goal

The Sprint Goal describes a fully working CRUD loop on the board. What actually exists is a plan for 2 of the 5 pieces needed to get there. No card can currently be seen, added, moved, edited, or deleted, because no implementation work has started.



\## Why

Sprint 1 time was spent almost entirely on planning and Scrum/Asana process work (Assignments 2.1 through 2.4) rather than on writing Flutter code. That's a legitimate use of early sprint time, decomposing "move a card" into subtasks during Assignment 2.3 is exactly the kind of prep that should happen before coding, but it means the sprint is ending with plans, not an increment.
